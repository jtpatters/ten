# M1 — Vertical Slice: Design and Plan

**Status:** v2 · 2026-11-07 · implements SPEC §16 M1. v2 folds in a three-lens design review
(rules and pacing, architecture and determinism, AI/UX/rendering).
**Goal:** Ancient era, 1 human vs 1 AI on a Duel map. Map gen, settle, grow, produce, 10 techs,
6 units, combat with preview, fog, a basic AI that expands and attacks, save/load and autosave.
**Exit:** the fun check — are the first 50 turns enjoyable?
**Decisions (the user, 2026-11-08):** turns 1–50 stay a peaceful exploration-and-expansion phase
(no raiders: they are M2), with two sources of things to react to: tribal villages to find while
exploring (§7.7), and an AI that shows itself from turn 30 (it contests land and stages troops in sight,
§13). A new city starts with 40 production (§5). The 10-tech tree, spent by about turn 54, is
accepted for M1; a longer research arc and gold sinks are M2. The home-screen name is
"10,000 Years".

This document fixes M1's rules, numbers, architecture and work split. Numbers are starting
values: they live in rules data (§12.2) and are tuned with HeadlessSim (§17). Where this document
and SPEC.md disagree for M1, this document wins; SPEC.md stays the long-term design.
Words: **own** = the acting player's; **foreign** = any other player's; **enemy** = a player you
are **at war** with.

---

## 1. Scope

**In M1**
- New game: map size (default **Duel**), seed, your civ; one human plus one AI per remaining civ
  slot (Duel 1+1, Small 1+3, Standard 1+5, Large 1+7). Standard speed only.
- Cities: found, claim territory, work tiles automatically (per-city focus), grow, produce from a
  queue, buy with gold, grow borders with culture, 5 buildings plus the Palace, take damage,
  bombard, be captured or razed.
- Yields: Food, Production, Gold, Science, Culture.
- 10 techs, 6 units (Settler, Builder, Scout, Warrior, Archer, Spearman), 3 improvements
  (Farm, Mine, Pasture).
- Combat: melee, ranged and city combat, preview, zone of control, fortify, healing, civilian
  capture, unit upkeep.
- Minimal diplomacy: meet, peace, war, peace proposals; closed borders at peace.
- Victory: domination, last civ standing, score at turn 250. Defeat on elimination.
- Fog with per-player memory (last-seen cities, borders, improvements, tribal villages, enemy
  units).
- Tribal villages on unclaimed land: the first player to reach one, with a unit or with a city's
  border, gets Gold, Science or a Builder (§7.7).
- A basic, fair AI (decides only from its player's knowledge) that explores, expands, builds,
  researches, defends, declares war and attacks; runs off the main thread.
- Save/load, autosave, Continue.
- HeadlessSim AI-vs-AI autoplay with batch statistics and a cross-process determinism check.

**Not in M1:** civ abilities (civs have names, leaders and city names only), resources, housing,
stability, loyalty, quarters, trade, minor factions, raiders, civics and policies, religion,
notable figures, wonders, ages, promotions (XP is tracked), roads, naval units and embarking,
undo, iCloud, tutorial, Archive, audio, game speeds, difficulty levels.

## 2. Game setup

- Civs, in this order: **Rome** (Trajan), **Mongols** (Genghis Khan), **Mali** (Mansa Musa),
  **China** (Wu Zetian), then Inca (Pachacuti), Khmer (Jayavarman VII), Norse (Harald Hardrada),
  Abbasid Caliphate (Harun al-Rashid). The human picks a civ; AIs take the others in order. Player
  *n* uses player colour *n*. Each civ has 12+ historical city names; after the list runs out,
  names continue "New <first>", "New <second>", …
- **All starts on one landmass** (no embarking in M1). MapGen places every start on the largest
  passable landmass with minimum distance 6. If it cannot, it regenerates with derived seeds
  `SplitMix64(seed: seed, salts: attempt, 0x57A7)` for up to 20 attempts, then lowers the minimum
  distance step by step to 3 on the best attempt. Players are never placed on separate
  landmasses. The map's **seed shown to the player stays the one they chose**.
- Start sites favour land the capital can work and improve: forest adds nothing over the bare
  tile, and each tile within 2 where a Farm, Mine or Pasture may stand adds a bonus (up to 8
  tiles, more next to the centre).
- Each player starts with a Settler, a Warrior and a Scout, and **10 Gold**.
- Tribal villages are placed after the start units, from the rules data (§7.7): the same number
  on every start's side.
- All players start at peace and unmet.

## 3. Turns

A **turn** (numbered from 1) has one **phase per living player**, in player order (the human is
player 0, so they always move first). `GameState.currentPlayer` is whose phase it is;
`GameState.phaseCount` counts every phase started so far. Only the current player may issue
commands (`notYourTurn`). `endTurn` ends the phase; after the last living player's phase the turn
number increases. If the current player is eliminated during their phase, the phase ends at once.

**Phase start** for player P, in order (empty steps are no-ops):
1. Expire P's own unanswered peace proposals (cool-down, §9).
2. Cities of P, in id order: assign citizens (§4) → collect yields → food and growth → production
   (§5) → culture and borders → healing and wall repair (§5) → clear the bombard flag.
3. Gold: add the gold yield, then pay upkeep (§7.1).
4. Science (§6).
5. Units of P, in id order: clear `zocStopped`; `fortifying` becomes `fortified`; clear `skipped`;
   heal (§7.5); restore moves; move military units out of territory closed to them (§9); then
   continue standing move orders.
6. Record P's phase start (`Player.lastPhaseStart = phaseCount`).
7. Update visibility, memory and "met" (§11), elimination and victory (§10).

Each player sees their finished production and research at the start of their own phase.

Base move cost is 1; a unit may always enter a passable tile if it has any moves left. **Terrain

**Improvements** (Builders, §7.2; never on forest, jungle or marsh tiles or city centres):

| Improvement | Raw | Effect | Allowed on | Tech |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Mine | 2 | +2 Prod | Hills, not Snow | Mining |
| Pasture | 3 | +1 Prod, +1 Gold | flat Grassland, Plains, Tundra | Animal Husbandry |

**City centre:** the tile's yields (without feature), Food raised to at least 2 and Production to
at least 1, then **+1 Production, +1 Science, +1 Culture**.
**Per city:** +1 Science per 2 population.
**Palace** (free in your first city): +2 Prod, +3 Gold, +3 Science, +1 Culture.

**Working tiles.** Each citizen works one tile the city owns within distance 3 of its centre
(not mountains or ice, not a tile an enemy unit stands on, not a tile another city works).
Citizens are assigned one at a time. Each takes the free tile with the highest focus-weighted
value among tiles that keep the city's **food surplus** (centre + tiles chosen so far + buildings
− 2 × citizens so far) at or above the focus floor. If no tile meets the floor it takes the tile
with the most Food (ties: weighted value, then distance, then tile index). Other ties: closer to
the centre, then lower tile index. A citizen with no free workable tile is **unemployed**: it eats
2 Food and yields +1 Production.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Balanced (default) | 3 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | +2 |
| Food | 5 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | +2 |

## 5. Cities

**Founding.** A Settler founds a city on its tile if the tile is passable land, not an oasis,
not owned by another player, and at distance ≥ 4 from every city centre. The city starts at
population 1 with **40 stored progress** (a Warrior's cost) and owns its centre plus every unowned
tile at distance 1; a tribal village on a tile it claims goes to the founder (§7.7). A player's
first city is
their **original capital** and gets the Palace. A rejected `foundCity` gives a generic "site not
valid" reason that never names a city the player cannot see (and that city is written into the
player's memory).

**Growth.** Each citizen eats 2 Food; the surplus goes into the food store. At
`threshold(p) = 11 + 8(p−1) + isqrt((p−1)³)` (11, 20, 29, 40, 51, 62, …) the city grows by 1 and
keeps the overflow. If the store would go below 0, the city shrinks by 1 (never below 1) and the
store becomes 0.

**Production.** A queue of up to 6 items (`unit(type)` or `building(id)`). Each phase:
- The phase's production goes to the first item that **can** complete. An item **waits** while it
  cannot: a Settler in a city of population 1, or a unit with no tile to spawn on. A waiting item
  keeps progress up to its cost. A Settler **first in the queue** that waits for population still
  takes the production and stored progress up to its cost (the rest stays stored) and completes
  in the first phase the city has grown (food comes before production).
- **At most one item completes per city per phase.** Its overflow carries to the next item,
  capped at that phase's production.
- With an empty queue, or only waiting items that take nothing, production goes to **stored
  progress**, capped at 2 × the city's production per phase (the rest is lost; progress already
  stored above the cap stays). Stored progress goes into the next item queued.
- A Settler costs 1 population when it completes.
- An empty queue is an attention item.

**Spawning.** A finished unit appears in the city if its slot is free (one military and one
civilian unit per tile); otherwise on the nearest tile the owner owns within distance 2 where it
may stand (ties: lower index).

**Buying.** `purchase(city, item)` costs **3 × the item's production cost** in Gold and completes
it at once (spawn and Settler rules apply; it does not count as the phase's completion). Buying a
building that is queued removes it from the queue and moves its progress into stored progress.

**Buildings** (each at most once per city; cannot be queued twice or once built):

| Building | Cost | Tech | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monument | 30 | — | +2 Culture |
| Granary | 55 | Pottery | +2 Food |
| Library | 70 | Writing | +3 Science |
| Walls | 60 | Masonry | 100 wall HP; +5 City Strength while wall HP > 0 |
| Market | 80 | Currency | +3 Gold |

**Borders.** Culture goes into a culture store. At `10 + 6n` (n = tiles this city has gained from
culture) the city claims one tile and keeps the overflow. Candidates: unowned tiles at distance
≤ 3 that touch the city's territory. Score `3·Food + 2·Prod + 2·Gold` (+2 with a river); mountains
and ice score 0 and are claimed only when nothing else is left. Ties: smaller distance, then lower
index. A tribal village on the claimed tile goes to the city's owner; a claim at the owner's phase
start comes before that phase start's gold, science and unit steps (§3, §7.7).

**City combat.** HP 200. **City Strength** = 14 + 2·population, +5 while wall HP > 0, +3 on hills,
+4 with a military unit on the tile (the **garrison**). A city is attacked as a whole; the garrison
cannot be targeted separately but may attack normally (a melee garrison that kills its target
stays in the city). Once per phase a city may **bombard**: a ranged attack at distance ≤ 2 with
City Strength. Healing: at its owner's phase start, if the city was not attacked since its owner's
previous phase start (`city.lastAttackedPhase < owner.lastPhaseStart`), it heals 20 HP and walls
regain 10 wall HP (walls at 0 wall HP still exist and recover).

**Capture.** A melee or antiCavalry unit (§8.3) attacking a city with 0 HP captures it without
combat: the city loses 1 population (never below 1); the garrison is destroyed; civilians on the
tile are captured (§7.3); Walls are destroyed; the Palace is removed; territory and other
buildings transfer; the queue and stored progress are cleared; HP becomes 50. In the phase it was
captured, the captor may `razeCity` it unless it is an original capital: the city is removed and
its tiles become unowned (improvements stay). A player who loses their capital gets the Palace in
their largest remaining city (ties: lower id); regaining their original capital moves it back.

## 7. Units

| Unit | Class | Cost | Strength | Ranged | Range | Moves | Sight | Tech |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Settler | civilian | 60 | — | — | — | 2 | 2 | — (completes at pop ≥ 2; −1 pop) |
| Builder | civilian | 40 | — | — | — | 2 | 2 | — (3 charges) |
| Scout | recon | 30 | 10 | — | — | 3 | 3 | — |
| Warrior | melee | 40 | 20 | — | — | 2 | 2 | — |
| Archer | ranged | 60 | 15 | 25 | 2 | 2 | 2 | Archery |
| Spearman | antiCavalry | 65 | 25 | — | — | 2 | 2 | Bronze Working |

`UnitType` stays a Swift enum (each type needs its own procedural art), in this order:
`settler, builder, scout, warrior, archer, spearman`. Every number lives in rules data;
`UnitType.isCivilian` stays hard-coded and validation checks it matches the data's class.
**Military** = every class except civilian. **Recon** units count for upkeep and zone of control,
attack units and capture civilians, but never attack cities and never count toward an AI army.
Spearmen get +10 against class `cavalry` (none exists in M1; the rule is data).

### 7.1 Upkeep
Each military unit beyond `2 + number of cities` costs 1 Gold per turn. At phase start (§3 step
3): while the treasury would be negative and the player has more military units than the free
allowance, disband the cheapest (then highest id) and recompute; if it is still negative, set it
to 0.

### 7.2 Builders
`buildImprovement(unit, kind)`: the tile must be owned by the builder's player, not a city centre,
unimproved, and satisfy the improvement's rule and tech. It completes at once and uses one charge
and all remaining moves; at 0 charges the builder is removed.

### 7.3 Civilians
Civilians have no strength and are never attack targets (no ranged attack or bombard on a tile
with only civilians). A military unit (any class) captures enemy civilians by moving onto their
tile at normal cost: each captured unit is removed and the captor gets a **new** unit (new id,
0 moves) — a Settler becomes a Builder with 2 charges; a Builder keeps its charges.
`Unit.owner` never changes.

### 7.4 Activities
`fortify` (military): `fortifying`, which becomes `fortified` (+4 defence) at the owner's next
phase start. `sleep` (any unit) until woken, moved or an enemy comes within 2. `skip`: no attention
needed for the rest of this phase. Moving or attacking clears any activity.

### 7.5 Healing
At its owner's phase start, a unit that neither moved nor attacked during its owner's previous
phase (`lastActedTurn < turn − 1`) heals: +20 in an own city, +15 in own territory, +10 in neutral
land, +5 in foreign territory (max 100).

### 7.6 Experience
Attacking gives +5 XP (melee) or +3 (ranged); surviving an attack gives +3. No effect in M1.

### 7.7 Tribal villages
**Tribal villages** stand on unclaimed land. The first player to reach one, with a unit or with a
city's border, gets the villagers' gift, and the tribal village is gone. They belong to no one,
never move, never fight and never lie on owned land. Every number is rules data (the optional
`tribalVillages` section, §12.2); without that section a game has none.

- **Placement.** `GameState.newGame` places them when `placeTribalVillages` is true (the App's new
  games and HeadlessSim pass true). It runs after the start units, from one
  `SplitMix64(seed: seed, salts: 0x4EA7)` stream.
  - **Steps.** Distances are land steps: breadth-first over passable land from each start, each
    step counting 1.
  - **Allowed tiles.** Passable land reachable from the starts; not a start tile; not Oasis or
    Snow; no unit on it; at least 4 hexes from every tribal village already placed.
  - **Own start and margin.** A tile's own start is its nearest (ties: lower player). Its margin is
    the steps to the next-nearest start minus the steps to its own.
  - **Own rounds.** One near round, 4–6 steps with margin ≥ 3 (relaxed: 3–7, ≥ 2); then two home
    rounds, 7–11 with margin ≥ 3 (relaxed: 6–12 ≥ 2, 5–13 ≥ 1, 4–14 ≥ 0). A round draws a start
    offset and visits the starts from it; each takes a random allowed tile on its own side from
    the first band that has one. A round stops at the first start with none: all its picks are
    dropped and the tiles they blocked are freed, so every start always has as many on its side as
    the others. Draws already made are not undone.
  - **Shared.** Then 1 more per start, each a random allowed tile ≥ 7 steps from every start with
    margin ≤ 1, while any is left.
  - **Order.** Candidates in tile-index order; draws in the order above.
  - **Counts.** A Duel map has 8 on most seeds.
- **Taking.**
  - **By a unit.** Entering its tile takes it: any move, a standing order's step or an Explore
    leg, passing through included. A move cut short takes only the tiles it entered. The takes
    come after the move's own events (civilians are captured first), in path order.
  - **By a city.** A city takes those on tiles it claims (§5).
  - **Nothing else** puts a unit on one: spawns land on owned tiles, and eviction skips them (§9).
- **Gifts.** Each player draws from their own bag of three gifts, Gold, Science and a Builder,
  shuffled again for every three gifts (`SplitMix64(seed: seed, salts: player, bag, 0x4EA8)`,
  Fisher–Yates). After every third gift, a player has had one of each.
  - **Gold:** +50.
  - **Science:** +20 research progress toward the current research; with nothing being
    researched it is banked (§6). With every tech known, the gift is the Gold gift instead.
  - **Builder:** a new Builder (3 charges) on the tribal village's tile, else on the nearest tile
    within 2 where it may stand (ties: lower index); with no room, the gift is the Gold gift
    instead. It is a civilian, so it costs no upkeep (§7.1).
  - **Timing (§3).** After a unit's take or a founding, the Science counts at the player's next
    phase start and the Builder has no moves left. A border claim at its owner's phase start comes
    before that phase start's gold, science and unit steps: its Science counts in that phase start,
    and its Builder gets its moves.
- **Events.** `tribalVillageTaken` goes to the taker and to players who see the tile;
  `tribalVillageGift` goes to the taker only, so other players never learn a gift.
- **Knowledge.** A player knows a tribal village only from seeing its tile (memory, §11).
- **Explorers.** A known tribal village within `seekTurns` (3) turns' moves is an explorer's first
  goal: for the AI (§13), the Explore order (§16) and HeadlessSim's newbie (§17) alike.

## 8. Combat

### 8.1 Strength
Effective strength = base (Strength; Ranged when attacking at range; City Strength for cities)
plus these modifiers, each listed in the preview:
- **Defender terrain** (units only): hills +3, forest or jungle +3, marsh −2.
- **Fortified** defender (units only) +4.
- **Support** (only against melee attacks, units only): defender +2 per other own military unit
  adjacent to it, max +6. (A melee attacker sees every neighbour of its target, so this never uses
  hidden information.)
- **Flanking** (melee attacks on units or cities): attacker +2 per other own military unit
  adjacent to the target, max +6.
- **River:** melee attack across a river edge (on a unit or a city) −5.
- **Wounded** (units): −1 per 10 HP missing.
- **Class bonus** from rules data.

### 8.2 Damage
`Δ = attacker − defender`, clamped to [−50, 50]. `base(Δ) = round(30 · e^(Δ/25))`, stored as a
**hard-coded integer table** (a test checks it against the formula). One roll `r` uniform in
[−15, 15] per direction; damage = `(base · (100 + r) + 50) / 100`. Rolls come from
`SplitMix64(seed: gameSeed, salts: turn, attackerKey, defenderKey, 0xC0B)` (attacker's roll first,
then retaliation), where a unit's key is its id and a city's is `1 << 32 | id`.

### 8.3 Attacks
- **Melee** = a unit with no Ranged value. **Ranged** = a unit with a Ranged value (target at
  distance ≤ range; no line of sight in M1). Cities attack with `cityAttack` (bombard).
- A unit attacks if it has moves left (`zocStopped` units included, §8.5); attacking ends its
  moves and clears its activity.
- **Targets:** an enemy military unit or an enemy city, visible to the attacker. A tile with an
  enemy military unit and civilians is attacked through the military unit; the civilians are
  captured if the attacker moves in.
- **Melee on a unit:** the defender takes `damage(Δ)`, the attacker `damage(−Δ)`. If the defender
  dies, the attacker survives with at least 1 HP and moves in (capturing civilians), unless it is a
  garrison. Otherwise, if the attacker reached 0 HP, it dies.
- **Ranged:** the defender takes `damage(Δ)`; no retaliation.
- **Cities** take damage on wall HP first, overflow to HP; HP never drops below 0. Melee attacks
  on a city get retaliation as above (the attacker can die). Only melee and antiCavalry units can
  capture (attacking a 0-HP city); ranged attacks never capture.

### 8.4 Preview and attack options
- `attackOptions(unit)` lists every target attackable **this phase**, each with the tile to attack
  **from**: the reachable tile adjacent to (melee) or within range of (ranged) the target with the
  lowest path cost (ties: lower index); `from` is the unit's tile when no move is needed. Cities:
  targets within 2.
- `combatPreview(attacker: Combatant, target: HexCoord, from: HexCoord)` with
  `enum Combatant { case unit(UnitID), city(CityID) }` returns: both effective strengths with every
  modifier by name and value; damage dealt and taken (min–max, r = −15 and +15); **kill chance**
  (units: the defender dies; cities: HP reaches 0) and **death chance**, computed exactly over all
  31 × 31 roll pairs; whether it captures (city already at 0 HP, or civilians).
- The UI and the AI choose attacks only from previews. The AI never applies commands to a scratch
  state to look at outcomes.

### 8.5 Zone of control
A land unit that enters a tile adjacent to an enemy military unit or enemy city has its movement
ended for the phase (`zocStopped`; `moveUnit` is rejected with `zoneOfControl`), but it may still
attack if it has moves left. Pathfinding treats such tiles as turn-ending, using only enemies the
player can see.

### 8.6 Movement and knowledge
Paths and move validation use the moving player's knowledge: unexplored tiles count as passable
land with cost 1; hidden units and cities are ignored, including at the goal. Moves execute step by
step and stop (clearing the order, event `moveInterrupted`) at the first tile that turns out to be
impassable or occupied, and stop for zone of control. Units never pass through or stop on tiles
with foreign units, and never enter a foreign city tile except by capturing it.

## 9. Diplomacy (minimal)

Each pair of players has a relation: `met`, `stance` (peace or war), `since` (turn of the last
change, or none), a pending peace proposal (from whom) and a proposal cool-down.
- Players **meet** when one sees the other's unit, city or territory (updated after every command).
- **declareWar(on:)**: requires met and peace; after a peace treaty, 10 turns must pass.
- **proposePeace(to:)**: requires war for ≥ 5 turns, no pending proposal and no cool-down. The
  target answers with **respondToPeace(from:accept:)** in its own phase. Declined or unanswered
  (expired at the proposer's next phase start) proposals start a 10-turn cool-down for that
  proposer. Peace sets `since`.
- **Closed borders at peace:** military units may not enter tiles owned by a player they are at
  peace with (civilians may). Military units inside such territory when peace is signed, or when a
  border grows under them, move at their owner's next phase start to the nearest tile they may
  stand on outside it that holds no tribal village (ties: lower index).
- At peace: no attacks and no zone of control.

## 10. Victory and defeat

- **Elimination:** after any command, a player with no cities and no Settlers is eliminated (units
  removed).
- **Domination:** a player wins when they own every other player's original capital (counting
  players who founded a city) and every player who never founded a city is eliminated. The last
  player standing also wins.
- **Score:** at the end of turn 250 the highest score wins (ties: lower player index).
  Score = 5·cities + population + 3·techs + 2·buildings (Palace excluded).
- If every human player is eliminated the outcome is **defeat**; all-AI games (HeadlessSim)
  continue to a victory or turn 250.
- With an outcome set, commands are rejected (`gameOver`).

## 11. Fog and knowledge

- **Visible** to a player: tiles within each own unit's sight, within 2 of each own city centre,
  and every tile the player owns. Stored per player (`Player.visible`) and recomputed only for
  players whose units, cities or territory a command changed.
- **Explored:** every tile ever visible.
- **Memory**, per player, updated after every command for newly visible tiles and for visible
  tiles whose owner, improvement, city or tribal village changed:
  - per tile: last-seen owner (`[UInt8]`, 0 = none, else player + 1) and improvement (`[UInt8]`,
    raw value, 0 = none);
  - cities ever seen (sorted by id): id, name, owner, position, population, walls, capital, HP,
    wall HP, turn seen; removed when the tile is seen without a city;
  - tribal villages seen (sorted tile indices; absent when none): added when the tile is seen with
    one, removed when it is seen without, and at the take by those who see the tile as its command
    begins (even when that command takes their sight away); one taken out of sight stays
    remembered;
  - **unit sightings** (foreign military units, sorted by id): id, owner, type, last tile, HP,
    turn seen; refreshed while visible, dropped after 20 turns or when seen killed.
- **PlayerView** (`state.view(for:)`) is the only way the UI and AI read the world: visible and
  explored bitsets, own units and cities in full, foreign units only on visible tiles, memory,
  relations, public rules and scores. Every query in §12.6 is a PlayerView method.
- The renderer gets cities, territory, improvements and tribal villages only from PlayerView
  (through the App, §15); diagnostic mode (fog off) shows live state for the human. The live list
  of tribal villages is internal to GameCore: the UI and the AI know one only from memory.

## 12. Architecture

### 12.1 Modules

```
GameCore      model, rules model + bundled rules data, engine, mechanics, PlayerView queries
MapGen        + same-landmass starts
MapRendering  + cities, borders, improvements, overlays, new unit art, combat animation, picking
GameAI        new: the M1 AI (depends on GameCore)
Persistence   new: save files (depends on GameCore)
HeadlessSim   + per-phase loop, AI autoplay (GameCore, MapGen, GameAI)
App           SwiftUI shell, HUD, panels, PlayerView → renderer adapter; links everything
```

M1 keeps rules **data inside GameCore** as package resources (`.copy("Rules")`, files at
`Sources/GameCore/Rules/*.json`, loaded with
`Bundle.module.url(forResource:withExtension:subdirectory: "Rules")`), so every module and test can
use `Ruleset.standard` without a dependency cycle. HeadlessSim runs from `.build/<config>/`.

### 12.2 Rules data
- `Ruleset`: a `final class`, immutable (`let` only, all lookup tables built in `init`),
  `Sendable`. Holds terrains, elevations, features, river, improvements, units (one per
  `UnitType`), buildings, techs (array order = tree order), civs (name, leader, city names) and
  constants (growth, culture, combat, upkeep, purchase, healing, score, turn limit, AI tuning).
  An optional top-level `tribalVillages` section holds every tribal village number (§7.7): bands,
  spacing, gifts and `seekTurns`. Without it a game has none, and the content hash is the one the
  rules had before the section existed.
- Identity: `id` (a hand-bumped string such as `"m1.1"`) plus `contentHash` (FNV-1a over the
  canonical re-encoding of the decoded rules, not file bytes).
- JSON uses arrays and integers only (ratios as percentages, e.g. `warRatioPct: 130`).
- Building, tech and civ IDs are string-backed `Codable` wrappers. `UnitType` and `Improvement`
  are enums whose defs come from the data (keyed by case name).
- Validation on load: every enum case has exactly one def, no dangling references, acyclic tech
  graph, numbers in range, `isCivilian` matches.

### 12.3 State
- `GameState` keeps synthesized `Codable`/`Hashable` by storing `rulesRef: RulesRef` and exposing
  `var rules: Ruleset { rulesRef.rules }`. `RulesRef` encodes `{id, contentHash}`; decoding takes the
  ruleset from `decoder.userInfo[.ruleset]` (default `.standard`), throws `RulesMismatch` on a
  different id, and warns on a different content hash. `==` compares id and content hash.
- New state: `currentPlayer`, `phaseCount`, `cities` (sorted by id), territory `tileCity: [Int32]`
  (city id + 1 per tile, 0 = none), `relations` (one per pair i < j, in pair order), `outcome`,
  `nextCityID`, `mapSizeName`, `tribalVillages` (internal to GameCore: the sites as sorted tile
  indices and each player's gift count; absent in a game without them). **No `Set` or `Dictionary`
  anywhere in the state.**
- `Tile.improvement: Improvement?` with `enum Improvement: UInt8 { case farm = 1, mine = 2,
  pasture = 3 }` (raw values append-only).
- `Player`: `civ`, `isAlive`, `gold`, research (current, target, progress, known techs),
  `originalCapital`, `capital`, `visible`, memory (§11), `lastPhaseStart`, name counter.
- `City`: id, name, owner, original owner, position, population, food store, queue, progress,
  stored progress, buildings (sorted), culture store, tiles gained, focus, HP, wall HP, founded
  turn, `lastAttackedPhase`, bombarded flag, worked tiles, captured turn.
- `Unit`: + `xp`, `activity` (`none, fortifying, fortified, sleeping, skipped`), `zocStopped`,
  `charges`, `lastActedTurn`. `owner` stays `let`.

**Frozen APIs** (used by the renderer, its tests, the App and HeadlessSim; a compile-only test in
MapRenderingTests touches each):
`GameState.newGame(seed:map:startPositions:humanPlayers:)` (rules, civs and `placeTribalVillages`

`Comparable`), `HexCoord`, `HexDirection` (order), `OffsetCoord`, `HexMap`,
`Tile.init(terrain:elevation:feature:riverEdges:)`, `Tile.landMovementCost`, `isLand`,

(current player only), `SplitMix64(seed:salts:)`, and MapGen's `MapGenerator.generate`,

### 12.4 Commands
`moveUnit`, `cancelOrders`, `endTurn`, `foundCity(unit)`, `buildImprovement(unit, Improvement)`,
`fortify(unit)`, `sleep(unit)`, `wake(unit)`, `skip(unit)`, `disband(unit)`,
`attack(unit, target: HexCoord)`, `cityAttack(city, target: HexCoord)`,
`setQueue(city, [ProductionItem])`, `purchase(city, ProductionItem)`, `setFocus(city, CityFocus)`,
`setResearch(TechID)`, `declareWar(on:)`, `proposePeace(to:)`, `respondToPeace(from:accept:)`,
`razeCity(city)`. Each is validated (typed `CommandError` with a reason the UI can show) and
applied by `GameEngine.apply(_:by:to:)`, which returns `[EventRecord]`.

### 12.5 Events
`EventRecord { event: GameEvent; audience: UInt16 }` — a player bitmask fixed when emitted, in a
fixed order (unit id, then city id). Events carry every hex they concern and the type and owner
of anything they remove: unit moved (per path), moveInterrupted, unit created, killed
(`unitKilled(unit:type:owner:at:)`), captured (`unitCaptured(old:new:type:at:)`), disbanded;
`combat(attacker:defender:from:to:ranged:dealt:taken:)`; city founded, grew, shrank, damaged,
captured, razed (`cityRazed(city:name:owner:at:)`); production completed; tech researched; border
grew; improvement built; players met; war declared; peace proposed, signed, declined; tribal
village taken (`tribalVillageTaken(at:player:unit:city:)`, by a unit or a claiming city); tribal
village gift (`tribalVillageGift(player:at:gift:)`, gift `gold(Int)`, `science(Int, toward:)` or
`builder(UnitID)`); player eliminated; game over; `phaseStarted(turn:player:)`.
Audience: tile events → players for whom a named tile is visible before or after the step (a
tribal village taken: also its taker); owner events (production, research, upkeep, tribal village
gifts) → the owner; diplomacy → both parties; war declared → every
player who has met either side; elimination and game over → everyone. The renderer, toasts and
attention queue show only records whose audience includes the viewer (diagnostic mode: all).

### 12.6 PlayerView queries (pure, deterministic)
City yields with breakdown by source; player income (gold, science, culture, upkeep);
production options (cost, turns, buy price, available or why not); research options; unit actions
(available or why not); `reachableTiles(unit)`; `attackOptions(unit)` and city attack targets;
`combatPreview`; tile yields; `settleScore(at:)` and `canFoundCity(at:)` (explored tiles only;
unexplored count 0); `movementField(unit) -> [Int32]` (array Dijkstra: move points to each tile
including turn ends, −1 unreachable, ZOC from visible enemies); `distanceField(from:) -> [Int16]`;
`dangerMap() -> [Int32]` (power of visible enemy units able to attack each tile next phase);
`garrison(of:)`; `power(of:)`; known territory, improvements, cities, sightings and tribal
villages (`knownTribalVillages`, `isKnownTribalVillage`, the viewer's own
`tribalVillageGiftsReceived`); score;
relation. Phase 2 may add read-only query files under `GameCore/Queries/`.

### 12.7 Determinism and performance
- All randomness from `SplitMix64` streams, each ending with its own domain salt (combat `0xC0B`,
  AI `0xA1`, map retries `0x57A7`, tribal village placement `0x4EA7`, tribal village gifts
  `0x4EA8`).
- No `Set` or `Dictionary` in the state; **no `Set`/`Dictionary` iteration and no `hashValue`**
  may affect state, events or command order.
- Integer arithmetic only for rules and AI scoring (no `Float`/`Double`, no `exp`).
- `stateHash()`: FNV-1a fed by a small internal `Encoder` (keys, integers and strings in
  synthesized-encoding order) — no JSON, so OS updates cannot change it. JSON is for saves only.
  Called at most once per phase in the App and HeadlessSim.
- Checks: in-process replay test; a golden-hash test for a short fixed command log (updated on
  purpose when rules change); `make determinism` runs `headless-sim --ai --seed 7 --turns 100`
  in two separate processes and compares the hashes.
- Budgets (Mac, release): visibility + memory ≤ 0.2 ms per command on Large; tribal village
  placement ≤ 5 ms per game on Large; AI phase ≤ 50 ms
  (max) on Duel and ≤ 250 ms on Standard 1+5 at turn 150. SPEC §12.3's device budget stands.
- Swift 6 strict concurrency. The AI runs in a background task on its own copy of the state.

## 13. AI (GameAI)

```swift
public enum AIPlayer {
    /// Plays `player`'s whole phase on `state`, ending with `.endTurn`. Deterministic.
    public static func playTurn(_ state: inout GameState, player: PlayerID) -> AITurnResult
}
public struct AIStep: Sendable { public var command: Command; public var events: [EventRecord] }
public struct AITurnResult: Sendable { public var steps: [AIStep] }
```

**Principles.** Decisions use only `state.view(for: player)` and its queries; state changes only
through `GameEngine.apply`. Randomness only from `SplitMix64(seed: seed, salts: turn, player,
0xA1)`. The AI is **stateless between phases**: posture, rival, target, rally city and army are
derived each phase from the state and its knowledge (relations, sightings, unit positions). All
thresholds below live in rules data. Fairness test: changing anything its player cannot see
(rival gold, research, queues, hidden units) leaves its commands identical.

**Power.** `power(u) = (ranged > 0 ? ranged : strength) × hp / 100`. Each city's garrison is its
lowest-id military unit on the tile. `army(P)` = Σ power of P's non-garrison, non-recon military
units. `estimate(R)` = Σ power of R's sightings ≤ 20 turns old + 15 × R's remembered cities.

**Posture** (each phase): **war** if at war with anyone; **prepare** if the war test below holds
for some rival except the army size; **expand** if a good site exists and cities < max cities
(Duel 6, Small 8, Standard 10, Large 12); otherwise **develop**.

**Research orders.** Expand/develop: Pottery, Animal Husbandry, Mining, Irrigation, Writing, Bronze
Working, Archery, Masonry, Sailing, Currency. Prepare/war/threatened: Mining, Bronze Working, Animal
Husbandry, Archery, Masonry, Pottery, Writing, Irrigation, Currency, Sailing.

**Cities** (utility, in this priority): defence quota first (1 military unit per city; 2 plus
Walls when a visible enemy, or any foreign military unit, is within 4 tiles); an escort for a
waiting Settler (Safety, below); a Warrior early for exploration if it has no Scout; Settlers
while a good site exists, cities < max cities, Settlers alive + queued < ⌈cities/2⌉ and no
visible enemy within 4 (at war, none in a city on or next to a tile an enemy unit could reach
next phase, or within 2 of a remembered enemy unit); Builders while worked tiles lack
improvements (≤ 1 builder per 2 cities);
Monument early; in prepare/war, an army of at least 1 Archer per 2 melee units once Archery is
known; then Granary, Library, Market. In prepare/war with ≥ 3 cities (`armyFirstCities`),
Builders and then the army come before Settlers, except in prepare while a good site exists and
100·(cities + Settlers alive + queued) < 125·R's remembered cities (`prepareSettlersRivalPct`).
An AI left with one city after losing one
builds, at peace, its Settler right after its garrison. Never start a military unit if net gold
after its upkeep would be < 0, unless at war and gold ≥ 10 × the deficit.
Buy a defender when threatened, or that escort, when gold ≥ price + 20; buy Settlers or Builders
only when gold ≥ 2 × price. Focus: Balanced.

**Settling.** First city: found on turn 1 where the Settler stands if valid, unless a neighbouring
tile scores ≥ 110% (then move and found on turn 2). **Good site:** explored, foundable by
knowledge, `settleScore ≥ aiMinSettleScore`, `movementField` cost ≤ 10 from the Settler and ≤ 8
tiles from an own city. Settlers choose in id order; a chosen site removes candidates within 3.
In prepare a Settler counts a site's score 2 lower per tile from R's nearest remembered city
(`prepareSiteRivalWeight`).
Re-evaluate every phase; retarget if a site turns invalid; with none left, sleep in the nearest own
city. **Safety:** a Settler or Builder never ends a phase within 2 tiles of a visible foreign
military unit, or of a remembered non-recon one out of sight (at war, for a visible enemy unit:
on any tile it could move onto in its next phase, with
full moves over the terrain the AI knows, ignoring units, borders and zones of control) unless an
own military unit shares its tile; if every route is unsafe it waits for an escort and they
travel together (an escort on its tile always travels with it). The nearest free military unit
within move cost 8 comes (to a neighbouring tile on its way when an own unit holds its tile); when
no free military unit can reach a waiting Settler, its nearest city (the one whose garrison is
nearest it, within 8) sends a free military unit standing by it, or else builds an escort first.
A Settler no escort could reach goes on alone, unless that ends where an enemy unit could reach it.
Once the military has moved, a Settler or Builder still where a visible enemy unit could reach it
steps to the least exposed tile it reaches (one an own military unit holds first), or else the
nearest free military unit that reaches its tile this phase steps onto it.

**Builders** improve the best worked unimproved tile in their territory (Farm by fresh water
first, Mines on hills, then Pastures), nearest first.

**Exploring.** Frontier = explored passable land tile next to an unexplored tile. A known tribal
village within `tribalVillages.seekTurns` (3) turns' moves comes first, before the frontier goal
and the rival hunt below (the cheapest by `movementField`, ties lower index, reserved like a
target), as for the Explore order (§16); an early Warrior exploring without a Scout follows the
same rule. Otherwise Scouts go to the
frontier with the lowest `movementField` cost (ties: lower index), choosing in id order and
reserving targets within 3 of each other. With no frontier left, or no target, a Scout first goes
to any known tribal village it can reach (at war, ending its phases only where the Explore order
may, §16); with none: disband Scouts while upkeep is
paid, otherwise sleep in the own city nearest the rival. If a met rival has no remembered city, a
Scout (built if needed) explores toward its remembered territory or latest sighting.

**War.** Declare war on rival R when all hold: turn ≥ 30; at peace with R for ≥ 10 turns (or never
at war); a remembered city of R exists; ≥ 2 ranged and ≥ 2 melee/antiCavalry non-garrison units
within 3 tiles of the rally city or 2 of the stage; `100·army ≥ 130·estimate(R)`. At most one
offensive war at a
time. R = the met rival with the highest `army / max(estimate, 1)` (ties: nearest remembered city).
**Target** = R's remembered city with the lowest path cost from the AI's capital; within ±2 cost
prefer non-capitals, then lower id. A city the AI founded that R now holds comes first while
`army` ≥ that city's estimate (15 + R's units sighted ≤ 20 turns ago within 4 tiles of it). **Rally** = own
city with the lowest path cost to the target. **Stage** (prepare): the tile an army unit may stand
on within the target's sight (2) with the lowest path cost from the rally city around closed
borders, else the one nearest the target; the army unit nearest it stands within 2 of it
(`prepareStageRadius`, `prepareStageUnits`), the rest gathers at the rally city.
The army advances when ≥ 4 army units are within 3 tiles of the rally city, or 8 turns after the
declaration.

**Tactics** (each phase, in order):
1. A melee/antiCavalry unit that can reach a 0-HP enemy city captures it.
2. Cities bombard, then ranged units fire: kills first, else the target left with the lowest HP;
   when ≥ 2 ranged units can hit the target city, they all fire at it.
3. Melee units attack a unit when expected damage dealt > taken or kill chance ≥ 50%. They attack a
   city when their maximum damage taken < their HP − 10 and either city HP ≤ 100 or ≥ 2 attacks
   already hit it this phase.
4. Units below 40 HP go to the nearest own city (else an own tile not adjacent to a visible enemy),
   fortify, and rejoin at ≥ 80 HP.
5. When an own city has ≤ 100 HP and an adjacent enemy: buy the best defender if affordable; the
   garrison attacks out only with kill chance ≥ 50%; army units within 5 tiles return.
6. At war, a military unit that can reach an unescorted enemy civilian this phase without ending
   next to 2+ visible enemies captures it first.
Otherwise army units move toward the rally city (in prepare, one toward the stage) or target;
garrisons fortify.

**Peace.** Propose or accept peace only after ≥ 10 turns of war, and only when
`100·army < 80·estimate(R)` or no city has changed hands (either side) for 30 turns.
**Captured cities:** keep; raze only population-1 non-capitals ≥ 8 tiles from every own city.

## 14. Persistence

```swift
public enum SaveKind: String, Codable, Sendable { case manual, auto, background }
public struct SaveSummary: Codable, Hashable, Sendable, Identifiable {
    // url (not stored in the file), name, kind, saved date, turn, current player,
    // civ name, map size name, seed, rules id, explorers (§16; stored only when any),
    // game (the game's id; stored only when set)
}
public actor SaveStore {
    public init(directory: URL)
    public func save(_ state: GameState, name: String, kind: SaveKind, explorers: [SavedExplorer] = [],
                     game: String? = nil) throws -> SaveSummary
    public func autosave(_ state: GameState, explorers: [SavedExplorer] = [], game: String? = nil) throws -> SaveSummary   // keeps the newest 10 `auto` per game
    public func saveBackground(_ state: GameState, explorers: [SavedExplorer] = [], game: String? = nil) throws -> SaveSummary // one slot per game, overwritten
    public func list() throws -> [SaveSummary]                       // newest first
    public func listUnreadable() throws -> [UnreadableSave]          // other versions, damaged files
    public func load(_ save: SaveSummary, rules: Ruleset) throws -> GameState
    public func delete(_ save: SaveSummary) throws
}
```

- **File:** `"TTYS"` | UInt16 LE format version | UInt32 LE header length | header JSON (summary)
  | LZFSE-compressed `GameState` JSON (`NSData.compressed(using: .lzfse)`). Written atomically.
  Autosaves named `auto-<turn>-<player>-<n>.ttys`; background slots
  `background-<seed>-<map size>-<civ>[-<game>].ttys`. A game is its id (`game`, given at New Game
  and carried by its saves; a save from before ids: its seed, map size and civ): the game
  autosaved keeps its newest 10 autosaves (by `n`, never by the clock), each other game its newest
  2 (40 in all), and the 3 newest other games keep their background slots. The save calls take the
  id (`game: String? = nil`).
- **Versions:** load only the current format version; bump it on any `GameState` schema change
  (a test compares a schema fingerprint — the sorted key paths of a fully populated state — to a
  constant). A new optional field that is left out while empty, so that older files decode as
  without it, keeps the version: the test lists it as an additive path of that version (the
  tribal villages of §7.7 are such fields). Refuse another rules id; load a different content hash
  with a warning. Files of another version (or damaged) are never pruned; Load lists them greyed,
  to be swiped away.
- A loaded state has the same `stateHash()` as the saved one under the same rules data. The hash
  covers the rules' content hash, so a file opened under changed rules (the warning above) hashes
  as its state does under those rules. A save from before tribal villages opens without any, and a
  game never gains them after it was made.
- **App policy:** autosave at the start of every human phase (after AI playback); background save
  on backgrounding and on Quit to Menu (during an AI phase it saves the state as of that phase's
  start; a finished game saves none). A debug scenario's game (and one loaded from its saves)
  writes neither, so it never prunes a real game's saves. Continue loads the newest save of any kind; Continue and Load
  open with the fog on (diagnostic mode is a New Game option). After any load, if the current
  player is an AI, its phases play (with normal playback) before input is enabled. Load from the
  game menu asks for confirmation if there are commands since the newest save. Default manual save
  name: "<Civ> – Turn N" (of the turn saved). Swipe to delete in the Load list, whose rows show map
  size and seed. Saves live in `Documents/Saves`; each save's last 50 notices (the Notices log,
  §16) in `Documents/NoticeLogs`, outside the save file, so a save without them opens with an
  empty log.

## 15. Rendering

The renderer stays a view. **Units** are still read from `GameState` (type, owner, hp, activity,
moves, orders), filtered to tiles visible to the viewer. **Cities, territory, improvements and
tribal villages** come only from renderer inputs, which the App builds from the viewer's
PlayerView (fog never leaks); MapRendering never reads cities or memory from `GameState`.

- `apply(state:events:)` sequences visible events: moves, then for combat a melee lunge or an arrow
  arc (bombards start at the city), hit flash (`onEventShown`), death fade on a ghost of the dead
  unit, the attacker's advance, capture recolouring. Foreign units' moves are trimmed to visible
  steps (fade in and out). Own units with no moves, an activity or a standing order are dimmed,
  except a Settler that may found a city where it stands (`setReadyUnits`).
- **Cities:** procedural settlements in the house style (ART.md), built from a kit of pieces
  (houses, towers, keep, wall segments, granary/library/market props) in a new **2048×512
  settlement atlas**; size tiers by population (1–2, 3–5, 6+), seeded variety, walls, capital keep;
  owner colour only on banners and trims (tinted white pieces). They draw in the unit layering so
  plants in front still cover their feet. Strategic zoom: a city token with the owner's emblem.
- **Borders:** a line in the owner's colour and ring pattern along territory edges, with a soft
  tint inside; drawn only where both tiles are explored. Never colour alone.
- **Improvements:** farm fields and pasture fences painted in the ground shader; mine entrances,
  spoil heaps and herd animals in the decor atlas's free slots. Per-tile improvement, territory and
  city-centre flags can live in spare bits of the existing tile texture.
- **Tribal villages:** two round huts and a hay pile from the settlement kit on a small trodden
  patch, with no banner, no owner colour and no name, so they never read as a small city; a thin
  pale smoke column while seen. Remembered ones take the fog look without smoke. At strategic
  zoom: a small ivory token. A shown `tribalVillageTaken` fades them out (about 0.6 s) and reports

## 17. HeadlessSim

- The main loop runs per phase: `--random` (today's stress mode, now random moves for the current
  player, then its `endTurn`; hashes once per turn) and `--ai` (every player is an AI).
- `headless-sim --ai [--games N] [--size duel] [--seed S] [--turns 150]` prints per game: outcome
  and turn; per player cities, population, techs, military, captures, tribal villages taken (by
  turn 25 and in all) and gift kinds; war turns; AI ms per phase (mean, max); final hash. Exits
  non-zero on a crash or a failed `GameState.checkInvariants()`. `--random` and `--ai` games place
  tribal villages; `--rules` with no `tribalVillages` section plays without them, bit-identical to
  the rules before them.
- `make headless` → `--random`; `make determinism` → two processes, same hashes.
- `--p0 scripted|turtle|newbie|shadow` puts a stand-in for a human in player 0's seat; early-game
  checks use `--p0 newbie --early` (cities, wars, Settler escort waits and tribal villages seen
  from that seat; a turn with a tribal village gift is not quiet). The newbie and the turtle walk
  to a known tribal village within `seekTurns` turns' moves before tapping the frontier;
  `--newbie-rush` makes the newbie's capital build a second Scout first.

**M1 balance targets** (Duel, 20 seeds, 150 turns): every AI founds ≥ 3 cities by turn 60 in ≥ 80%
of games; a war starts by turn 80 in ≥ 70%; a city is captured by turn 150 in ≥ 30%; no crashes;
deterministic.

**Tribal village targets.** These compare the standard rules with the same rules without the
`tribalVillages` section, on the same seeds (Duel, newbie seat, 120 seeds × 50 turns unless noted):
- quiet turns in turns 1–25 fall by ≥ 1.5 (turns 1–10 by ≥ 1.0), with at most 12 games quieter;
- the newbie's first gift by turn 5 in ≥ 60% of games, and by turn 8 in ≥ 80%;
- gifts by turn 25 between 2.0 and 3.5 on average, within 0.5 of the AI's;
- the 10th tech no more than 4 turns sooner;
- no newbie city lost by turn 80 (40 seeds); AI war on the newbie in at most 6 more of 240 games
  by turn 50 (10 more by turn 60);
- with the standard rules (40 seeds), a newbie whose capital builds a second Scout first gains at
  most 2.0 gifts by turn 25 over one that does not, while the AI's techs and cities at turn 50
  fall by at most 0.5 and 0.25;
- the Explore order's land explored by turn 20 falls by at most 5 points (`ExploreOrderPlayTests`);
- the balance targets above still met.
