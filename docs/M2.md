# M2 — Core 4X: Design and Plan

**Status:** draft v0.1 · 2026-11-13 · implements SPEC §16 M2. Builds on M1.md (v2), which stays
the reference for every rule not restated here.
**Goal:** Ancient → Medieval, 4 civs with abilities (Rome, Mongols, Mali, China), 1 human vs up to
7 AIs. Civics, governments and policies; Stability, Housing, quarters, Loyalty; strategic and
luxury resources; trade; minor factions; raiders; diplomacy basics; naval units and embarking;
promotions; AI layers 1–4 (SPEC §12.1). HeadlessSim already exists and is extended.
**Exit:** the **depth check**: is a full game (turns 1–150, Standard map, 1+5) interesting all the
way through, with a decision worth making every turn (SPEC pillar 2)? Plus the **art/audio
sourcing decision** (D11).

Same conventions as M1.md: numbers are starting values in rules data and are tuned with
HeadlessSim (§19); where this document and SPEC.md disagree for M2, this document wins.
**own** / **foreign** / **enemy** as in M1.md.

## 1. Scope

**In M2**
- **Eras:** Ancient, Classical, Medieval, with a global world era (§2).
- **Research:** 30 techs (the 10 M1 techs, 2 new Ancient, 7 Classical, 11 Medieval) with Insights (§6).
- **Civics:** 18 civics with Insights; 7 governments; 30 policy cards (§7).
- **Units:** 24 unit types (the 6 M1 types, 14 new, 4 unique units); upgrades; promotions;
  embarking and 4 naval units (§8, §9).
- **Cities:** Housing, Stability, 8 quarters with adjacency and placement mode, 18 buildings,
  Loyalty and Free Cities, governor focus extended (§5).
- **Map:** resources (8 bonus, 8 luxury, 2 strategic for M2: Horses, Iron), roads, 4 new

- **World:** raider camps and raiders (§11); minor factions, envoys, Patrons (§12); trade routes
  and Traders (§13).
- **Diplomacy basics** (§14) and **civ abilities** for Rome, Mongols, Mali, China (§15).
- **Victory:** domination, last civ standing, score at turn 150 (§16).
- **AI:** grand strategy, economy, military operations with influence maps, tactics (§17).
- **App:** tech and civic trees, government/policy screen, quarter placement mode, city panel

## 2. Eras and pacing

**Eras** have an index (`ancient = 0, classical = 1, medieval = 2`). Every tech and civic belongs
to one era (rules data).

**World era (M2-D10).** At each turn start (before player 0's phase), compute for each living
player `progress(P) = 100 · (cost of P's known techs of the world era) / (cost of all techs of the
world era)` (civics join this sum in 2.3). The world advances one era when the mean progress of
living players is ≥ `worldEraAdvancePct` (50; 60 put Medieval at turn 130+ in HeadlessSim), or
when any player knows ≥ `forceAheadCount` (3) techs of the era after the next one (not reachable
in M2, the rule is data). At most one era per turn start. On an advance:
- event `worldEraAdvanced(era)` to everyone; top-bar era meter resets;
- every player's credit (§6.2) toward each unknown tech and civic of the era just left rises by
  `catchUpInsightPct` (20) of its cost, capped at cost − 1;
- raider strength steps up (§11); minor factions get the era's defenders (§12).

**Pacing targets** (Standard map, 1+5 AI, HeadlessSim, mean over 20 seeds):

| Milestone | Target turn |
|---|---|
| World era Classical | 40–50 |
| World era Medieval | 80–95 |
| First civ knows every tech | 115–130 |
| Median civ knows every tech | ≤ 145 |
| First civic tree completed | 120–140 |

M1 data for reference (current build, 6 Duel + 3 Standard AI games, 250 turns): the 10-tech tree is
exhausted around turn 54; at turn 250 AIs have 6–10 cities and population 40–130.

## 3. Map

### 3.1 Resources
`Tile.resource: Resource?` (`enum Resource: UInt8`, raw values append-only). Placed by MapGen
after terrain, from `SplitMix64(seed: seed, salts: 0x2E50)`; a resource never changes or
depletes. A resource is **revealed** to a player by a tech (rules data); before that the player
neither sees it nor gets its yields or effects. Memory records last-seen resources only once
revealed (§3.4).

| Resource | Kind | Yield | On | Improvement | Revealed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wheat | bonus | +1 Food | flat Plains, Grassland, Floodplain | Farm | — |
| Rice | bonus | +1 Food | flat Grassland, Marsh | Farm | — |
| Cattle | bonus | +1 Food | flat Grassland | Pasture | — |
| Sheep | bonus | +1 Food | Hills (not Snow) | Pasture | — |
| Deer | bonus | +1 Prod | Forest, Tundra | Camp | — |
| Stone | bonus | +1 Prod | Grassland, Plains, Hills | Quarry | — |
| Fish | bonus | +1 Food | Coast, Lake | Fishing Boats | — |
| Bananas | bonus | +1 Food | Jungle | Plantation | — |
| Wine | luxury | +1 Gold | flat Plains, Grassland | Plantation | — |
| Silk | luxury | +1 Culture | Forest | Plantation | — |
| Spices | luxury | +2 Food | Jungle | Plantation | — |
| Incense | luxury | +1 Gold | Desert, Plains | Plantation | — |
| Ivory | luxury | +1 Prod | Plains, Grassland, Jungle | Camp | — |
| Furs | luxury | +1 Gold | Forest, Tundra | Camp | — |
| Salt | luxury | +1 Food, +1 Gold | flat Plains, Desert, Tundra | Mine (also flat) | Mining |
| Pearls | luxury | +1 Gold | Coast | Fishing Boats | — |
| Horses | strategic | +1 Food, +1 Prod | flat Grassland, Plains | Pasture | Animal Husbandry |
| Iron | strategic | +1 Prod | Hills, Plains, Grassland, Desert | Mine (also flat) | Bronze Working |

**Placement.** Per map size a target count per kind (Duel: 14 bonus, 8 luxury, 6 strategic;
scale by land tiles). Each start must have within 3 tiles: ≥ 2 bonus, 1 luxury; within 6: 1 Horses
or Iron (fairness pass, like M1's start scoring). A luxury type is placed in **clusters** of 2–4
so that trading them matters. No resource on a start tile, a tribal village or a city centre;
oasis and snow get none.

**Luxuries** give Stability (§5.2): each **distinct** improved luxury the player owns gives +1
Stability to its `luxuryCities` (4) cities with the lowest Stability, recomputed each phase start.
Luxuries can be traded (§14.3): a traded copy counts as owned by the receiver, and is lost to the
giver while the deal lasts, unless the giver owns more than one source.

**Strategic resources (M2-D5).** `Player.stockpile[resource]`: each improved source in the
player's territory adds +2 at phase start, up to `cap` (20; +10 when the world era is Medieval).
A unit that needs a resource costs `resourceCost` (10) from the stockpile when it completes or is
bought (the queue item waits otherwise, M1 §5 waiting rules) and when it upgrades. At war, every
such unit uses 1/turn from the stockpile; when the stockpile is 0 those units fight at −5 (shown in
the preview as "Out of Iron").

### 3.2 Embarking
- Land units may enter Coast and Lake tiles once their owner knows **Sailing**, and Ocean once
  they know **Cartography** (§6). Entering water from land costs all remaining moves; embarked
  units move 3 on water and leaving water to land costs 1.
- **Embarked** units: strength 10 (defence only), cannot attack, no ZOC, sight 2.
- Naval units: §9.4.
- MapGen: M1's "all starts on one landmass" stays for Duel; on Small and larger maps, Continents
  and Archipelago scripts may put starts on different landmasses (§3.5).

### 3.3 Roads
- **Road** is a tile flag (`Tile.road: Bool`), not an `Improvement` (a farm tile can also have a
  road).
- Built by: traders (§13), automatically, on each tile of their route's path; a **Builder**
  action that uses no charge and all moves (needs **The Wheel**); Rome's ability (§15).
- Moving along a road from one road tile to another costs ⅓ of a move (units have 3 move points
  per move internally: M1 moves × 3; every M1 cost is multiplied by 3, so nothing else changes)
  and ignores river crossing costs if **Engineering** is known. Roads are visible in memory like
  improvements.
- **Pillage** (any military unit, costs all moves): removes a foreign improvement or road at war;
  pillaging an improvement gives the pillager 25 Gold or heals it 50. The tile owner's Builder
  repairs it for one charge.

### 3.4 Knowledge
Memory gains per-tile `road` (bit) and `resource` (raw, 0 = none or unrevealed); a resource
revealed later fills in for every explored tile at once (revealing is knowledge of the map, not
new sight).

### 3.5 Map scripts
M2 adds the **Continents** and **Archipelago** scripts (SPEC §3.1) alongside M1's default
(which becomes **Pangaea**). Duel keeps all starts on one landmass; Small and larger may separate
starts on Continents/Archipelago. Fractal stays for M3.

As built (`MapScript`, `MapGenSettings.script`):
- **Pangaea** is M1's generator unchanged, and the default.
- **Continents** adds two ocean seams half a map apart, at a seeded phase, with at least 2 water
  tiles on each side. Land swells between the seams.
- **Archipelago** uses 3× finer noise (period 12) and 75% of the land fraction (about 28% land).
- **Starts:** off Duel, starts go to the landmasses with at least `minStartLand` (45) tiles per
  start. They are allocated by most land per start (D'Hondt) and keep the usual spacing, with
  the minimum distance also applied across landmasses.

## 4. Tiles, improvements and yields

### 4.1 New improvements
| Improvement | Raw | Effect | Allowed on | Tech |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quarry | 4 | +1 Prod | Stone | Masonry |
| Plantation | 5 | +2 Gold | Bananas, Wine, Silk, Spices, Incense | Irrigation |
| Camp | 6 | +1 Gold, +1 Prod | Deer, Ivory, Furs | Animal Husbandry |
| Fishing Boats | 7 | +1 Food | Fish, Pearls (water) | Sailing |
| Lumber Mill | 8 | +1 Prod (+1 more with Engineering) | Forest (no resource) | Construction |

Every improvement on a resource also enables its yield (bonus) or its effect (luxury, strategic);
an unimproved resource gives its yield only. M1's ban on improvements on forest, jungle and marsh
stays except for the rows above. Farm and Mine gain:
- **Farm:** +1 Food with the **Feudalism** civic for each adjacent Farm, max +1 (two farms side by side both
  get it).
- **Mine:** +1 Prod with **Apprenticeship**.

### 4.2 Builders
Builders keep 3 charges (+1 with the *Ilkum* policy, §7.3). M1 §7.2 rules otherwise; a Builder may
also build a road (§3.3) or repair a pillaged improvement (one charge).

### 4.3 Influence
A new player yield used for envoys (§12) and deal-making.
- Palace +1; **Chiefdom** and later governments give a base (§7.2); the *Diplomatic Service*
  civic's policies add more.
- Every `influencePerEnvoy` (25, then +5 per envoy sent so far, max 60) Influence gives one
  **envoy token**. Shown in the top bar.

### 4.4 City centre and yields
Unchanged from M1 except: **Palace** +1 Influence; a city on a river or next to a lake or
mountain counts as having **fresh water** (Housing, §5.2).

## 5. Cities

M1 §5 stands (founding, growth curve, queue, spawning, buying, borders, city combat, capture).
Changes and additions below.

### 5.1 Growth with Housing
**Housing** caps growth softly. A city's Housing:

| Source | Housing |
|---|---|
| Base: no fresh water / coast or lake, no fresh water / fresh water (§4.4) | 2 / 3 / 5 |
| Granary | +2 |
| Aqueduct quarter | +4 (+6 if the city has no fresh water) |
| Every 2 worked Farms, Pastures, Plantations or Camps | +1 |
| Palace | +1 |
| Policies, government, civ ability | §7, §15 |

The food surplus that goes into the food store is scaled by `pop` vs Housing (integer %):
`pop ≤ housing − 2` → 100%; `housing − 1` → 50%; `≥ housing` → 25%; `≥ housing + 4` → 0%.
A shrinking city (negative surplus) is never scaled. The city panel shows Housing as
"Pop 7 / Housing 8" with the growth scale.

### 5.2 Stability
A per-city integer. **Stability = supply − need**, recomputed at the owner's phase start before
yields.
- **Need:** `max(0, (pop − 1) / 2)` (pop 1–2: 0, 3–4: 1, 5–6: 2, …).
- **Supply:** luxuries (§3.1); Palace +1 in the capital; Arena quarter +2 to every own city within
  6 of it (not cumulative: the best one), its Arena building +1 more in the same reach; Monument
  +1 in its city; policies and government (§7); civ ability (§15).
- **Penalties** (subtracted from supply):
  - **Empire size:** −1 in every city per full 4 cities beyond the first 4 (8–11 cities: −1, 12–15: −2).
  - **Overcrowding:** −1 per population above Housing.
  - **War weariness:** −1 per full 100 weariness (§10.3), max −3.
  - **Occupied:** −2 in a city captured ≤ 10 turns ago.

| Stability | State | Effect on the city |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ +3 | Thriving | +10% to every yield except Food |
| 0 … +2 | Content | none |
| −1 … −2 | Restless | −10% to every yield except Food; growth scale ×½ |
| −3 … −4 | Unrest | −25% to every yield except Food; no growth |
| ≤ −5 | Revolt | as Unrest; every 5 turns a **rebel** (a raider unit of the current era, §11) spawns on a free tile within 2 |

Percentages apply to the city's yield total after buildings, rounded down. Loyalty uses Stability
(§5.5). A city below Content is an attention item, with the breakdown one tap away.

### 5.3 Quarters (M2-D3)
A **quarter** is a tile inside the city's territory, within 3 of its centre, that holds a
specialized building zone (SPEC §5.2). Rules:
- **Production item** `quarter(kind, at: HexCoord)`; the tile is fixed when queued (placement
  mode, §18.2). Only one of each kind per city; the Harbor and Aqueduct need their placement
  rule; Barracks cannot be adjacent to the city centre. Not on a city centre, mountain, ice, a
  resource tile with a strategic or luxury resource, or a tile with a tribal village. A quarter
  tile keeps its terrain but **loses its improvement**, is not workable, and blocks its tile from
  having roads removed.
- **Cap (D14):** `1 + pop / 3` quarters per city, Aqueduct exempt.
- **Cost:** `quarterBase (55) + quarterPerEra (30) × world era`, fixed when the item is queued.
  Aqueduct 50 flat. Production overflow and buying as for buildings; quarters cannot be bought
  with Gold (buildings in them can).
- **Unlocks:**

| Quarter | Unlocked by | Adjacency (rules data; halves summed, then rounded down) | Yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| Campus | Writing | +1 per adjacent mountain; +½ per adjacent jungle; +½ per adjacent quarter | Science |
| Market | Currency | +2 if on a river; +2 per adjacent Harbor; +½ per adjacent quarter | Gold |
| Workshop | Apprenticeship | +½ per adjacent Mine or Quarry; +2 per adjacent Aqueduct | Production |
| Harbor | Celestial Navigation | on Coast or Lake next to the city's land; +1 per adjacent sea resource; +2 if adjacent to the city centre | Gold |
| Barracks | Bronze Working | none | — (§5.3.1) |
| Theater | *Drama and Poetry* | +1 per adjacent wonder (none in M2); +½ per adjacent quarter | Culture |
| Arena | *Games and Recreation* | none | Stability (§5.2) |
| Aqueduct | Engineering | must touch the city centre and a river, lake or mountain | Housing (§5.1) |

- **Adjacency yield** is added to the city's yields every phase (shown by source). Placing a
  quarter can raise neighbours' adjacency; the placement preview shows both (§18.2).
- **Specialists:** each building in a quarter adds one **specialist slot**. A specialist yields
  +2 of the quarter's yield (Barracks: +1 Production, +1 Gold; Arena: +1 Culture, +1 Gold).
  Citizen assignment (M1 §4) treats a slot like a workable tile with those yields; the focus
  weights pick between tiles and slots.
- **Pillage:** an enemy military unit adjacent to (or on, for Harbor) a quarter may pillage it
  (all moves; +50 Gold to the pillager, or +25 HP). A pillaged quarter gives no adjacency,
  specialist or building effects until repaired: an automatic queue item at the front costing 25%
  of the quarter's cost.

#### 5.3.1 Barracks quarter
Defends itself: **HP 100**, strength = City Strength − 4 (§5.6 base, no garrison bonus), one
bombard per phase at range 2. It is attacked like a city but is never captured; at 0 HP it is
pillaged. Units trained in a city with a Barracks quarter start with +15 XP (+25 with its Armory).

| Monument | centre | 30 | — | +2 Culture, +1 Stability |

set to 0 and the player's science and culture fall by the shortfall that phase.

### 5.5 Loyalty
`City.loyalty` 0–100. At the owner's phase start, after Stability:
1. **Pressure.** For each living player X: `P(X) = Σ pop(c) × (10 − d)` over X's cities c within
   distance d ≤ 9 of this city (this city itself counts for its owner at d = 0). Free Cities and
   minor factions exert none.
2. **Delta** = `clamp((P(owner) − max foreign P) / loyaltyDivisor (20), −8, +8)` plus modifiers:
   - capital: +8 in the capital, +3 within 9 of it;
   - garrison (an own military unit on the centre) +2;
   - Stability: `+min(stability, 3)` when > 0; `max(stability, −5)` when < 0;
   - captured ≤ 10 turns ago: −3;
   - enemy military units on its tiles: −1 each, max −3;
   - policies and civ abilities (§7.3, §15).
3. **Clamp** the delta to [−10, +10] and add it; clamp loyalty to [0, 100].
- A founded city starts at 100; a captured one at 50; a city that flips at 50.
- **At 0** the city becomes a **Free City** (owner = the Free Cities faction, §12.5): its
  garrison is moved out as by closed borders (M1 §9), its queue cleared, its buildings and quarters
  kept. Event `cityRevolted`.
- **Free City flips:** each turn a Free City records the civ with the highest positive P; after
  `flipTurns` (5) consecutive turns with the same civ, it joins that civ. Or it can be conquered
  (it is always at war with every civ).
- **Warnings:** a city whose projected loyalty reaches 0 within 5 turns is an attention item; the
  banner shows the trend arrow and turns to flip; the Loyalty lens shows pressure sources.
- **Tuning targets (D15):** as SPEC §5.5; HeadlessSim checks them (§19).

### 5.6 City combat
M1 §5 stands; Castle adds wall HP and strength (§5.4). City Strength also gets +1 per
Medieval-era military tech known (the city keeps pace with units). **Siege** units (§9.2) deal
+50% damage to wall HP and ignore the Walls' strength bonus.

### 5.7 Governor focus
M1's four focuses plus **Science** (weights Food 2, Prod 1, Gold 1, Science 4, Culture 1, floor 0)
and **Culture** (2, 1, 1, 1, 4, floor 0). Specialist slots take part (§5.3).

### 6.2 Insights
Each tech and civic may have one **Insight** trigger (rules data: a trigger kind and arguments
from a fixed list — `foundCoastalCity`, `meetCiv`, `improveResource(kinds)`, `killWith(classes)`,
`buildImprovement(kind, count)`, `own(building|quarter|unit class, count)`, `ownLuxuries(count)`,
`quarterAdjacency(kind, min)`, `routes(count)`, `cityDefended`, …). When it fires, the player's **credit** for that
item rises by `insightPct` (40) of its cost, once, capped at cost − 1; event `insightGained`.
- **Credit model:** M1's single research pool stays (`researchProgress`, kept when switching).
  Credit is per item (`Player.techCredit`, tree order): a tech needs `cost − credit` science;
  learning it clears its credit.
- **Evaluation:** after each command (`GameEngine` before `finishCommand`), the command's events
  mark which trigger kinds may have changed for which players (`improvementBuilt` →
  `buildImprovements` for its owner, `playersMet` → `meetPlayers`, …); only those are evaluated,
  from the state after the command. Kills and defended cities are read from the events.
- **2.1a kinds:** `ownCoastalCities`, `meetPlayers`, `ownCities`, `buildImprovements`,
  `ownBuildings`, `ownUnits`, `killWith` (with optional `against`), `cityDefended`. Insights that
  need later systems (resources, quarters, trade routes, Stables, Water Mills, Harbors) are added
  to the data when those land; until then those techs have none.
The tree shows each node's Insight text and a ✓ once gained.

Insights are the main source of "every turn a decision" in the research arc: the tree shows which
Insights are one step from firing (attention item "Build a Mine to get the Insight for The Wheel"
only once, and only the first 10 turns of the game).

## 9. Combat

M1 §8 stands (strength, damage table, rolls, preview, ZOC, knowledge-aware moves). Additions:

### 9.1 Modifiers
New preview lines (each named, as in M1): promotions; government and policies; class bonuses
(§8.1); "Out of Iron/Horses" −5 (§3.1); embarked defender (fixed strength 10, no other modifiers);

### 9.2 Siege and walls
Damage to wall HP from `siege` units and from units with *Incendiaries* is ×150%; other units deal
×50% to wall HP while the city has wall HP (melee and cavalry included), and full damage once wall
HP is 0. (M1 had full damage to walls for everyone; M2 makes walls matter.) City bombard is

### 9.3 Zone of control
M1 §8.5 plus: embarked units exert none; naval units exert ZOC on water only, land units on land

### 9.4 Naval

- `navalMelee` may attack naval and embarked units, and coastal cities and Harbor quarters;
  capture is land-only (they cannot capture cities).
- `navalRanged` attack anything in range (ranged rules); −5 vs cities.
- Healing: +20 in an own city or adjacent to an own Harbor, else +0 at sea in M2.
- Embarked land units attacked by naval units take the damage table as usual with strength 10.

## 10. War

### 10.1 Declarations
M1 §9 rules stand, plus types (rules data), shown before confirming:
- **Surprise war:** at peace with no denouncement for 5+ turns: relationship −30 with the target,
  −10 with every civ who has met the target; war weariness ×1.5 for the declarer.
- **Formal war:** after denouncing the target ≥ 5 turns earlier (§14.2): −10 / −3.
- **Reconquest:** the target holds a city the declarer founded: −5 / 0.
War on the Free Cities is permanent and costs nothing; war on a minor faction follows §12.4.

### 10.2 Peace terms
`proposePeace(to:, terms: Deal)`: peace may carry a deal (§14.3): gold, gold per turn, cities
(not capitals), resources. The AI evaluates it like any deal.

### 10.3 War weariness
`Player.warWeariness`: +3 per own unit killed, +1 per own unit damaged in a phase, +5 per own city
attacked in a phase, +10 per own city lost, all ×1.5 after a surprise war; at peace −10 per turn
(min 0); at war −1 per turn. Stability penalty §5.2.

## 11. Raiders

Raiders are the **Raiders faction**: a non-player with units and camps, always at war with
everyone, never met, no diplomacy. It acts in its own phase after the last player's (so the
human still moves first each turn). Every number is rules data (`raiders` section; a game
without it has none, as with tribal villages).

- **Camps.** `RaiderCamp { tile, spawnedTurn, units }` on unowned passable land, never within
  `campMinCityDistance` (7) of a city, within 4 of another camp, or on a tribal village. Created
  from turn `firstCampTurn` (50) on a tile **no living player can see** at the moment of creation,
  every `campInterval` (10) turns while camps < `maxCamps` (Duel 2, Small 3, Standard 5, Large 7).
  Stream `SplitMix64(seed: seed, salts: turn, 0x2A1D)`.
- **Spawns.** A camp spawns one unit every `spawnInterval` (8) turns while it has fewer than
  `campUnits` (3) alive, of the **world era**'s raider set (Ancient: Warrior, Archer; Classical:
  Swordsman, Horseman; Medieval: Man-at-Arms, Crossbowman, Horseman). Raider units ignore
  strategic resources and have no upkeep.
- **Behaviour** (GameAI, `RaiderAI`, stateless):
  1. a unit with a visible civilian it can capture this phase captures it;
  2. attack a visible target when the preview's expected damage dealt ≥ taken (cities only when
     ≥ 2 raiders are adjacent);
  3. pillage an improvement it stands next to within 1 move, preferring roads and luxuries;
  4. otherwise roam: move toward the nearest visible player city within 8 of the camp; beyond
     that, return within 3 of the camp.
  Raiders never leave land, never take tribal villages, and never capture cities (a 0-HP city is
  pillaged: −1 population, −50 Gold to its owner, the city back to full HP, and the raider's
  moves end).
- **Clearing.** A player military unit entering a camp tile destroys it: +`campGold` (50, +25 per
  world era) Gold, event `raiderCampCleared`, Insight for *Military Tradition*.
- **Rebels** (Stability Revolt, §5.2) are raider units with no camp; they roam around their city.
- **Knowledge.** Camps are remembered like tribal villages; raider units like foreign units.
- **Targets (§19):** no raider unit within 6 of a start's city before turn 55; a newbie seat
  loses no city to raiders by turn 100 in 40 seeds.

## 12. Minor factions

### 12.1 Setup
Single-city factions (Duel 2, Small 4, Standard 6, Large 8 in M2; SPEC numbers in M3 once the
roster grows). Each has a **type** (Scientific, Cultural, Trade, Militaristic, Industrial; no
Religious in M2) and a name from a per-type list (rules data, 6 names each). MapGen places them
after the players, ≥ 6 from every start and ≥ 5 from each other. They start with a city of
population 2, Walls, a Warrior and an Archer, and get the world era's defenders when the era
advances (one melee and one ranged of the new era, replacing the oldest if over 4).

They are players in `GameState.players` with `kind: .minor` (humans and AIs are `.major`), so
units, cities, fog and events work unchanged. They take phases after the majors and before the
raiders; GameAI plays them with `MinorAI` (defend, build era units up to 4 military, never
settle, never declare war, accept peace whenever offered after 5 turns).

### 12.2 Envoys
`sendEnvoy(to: minor)` spends one envoy token (§4.3). A player who has met the minor may send
any number. Bonuses per player, by envoys at that minor:

| Envoys | Scientific | Cultural | Trade | Militaristic | Industrial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +2 Science in the capital | +2 Culture in the capital | +4 Gold in the capital | +2 Prod in the capital when building units | +2 Prod in the capital when building quarters or buildings |
| 3 | +2 Science in every city with a Library | +2 Culture in every city with an Amphitheater | +2 Gold in every city with a Market | +2 Prod in every city with a Barracks or Stable | +2 Prod in every city with a Workshop |
| 6 | +2 more (Universities) | +2 more (Arenas) | +2 more (Banks) | +2 more (Armories) | +2 more (Water Mills) |

**Patron:** the player with the most envoys, at least 3 (ties: whoever reached the count first).
The Patron gets the minor's improved luxuries and strategic sources as their own, sees its
territory, and may `levy(minor)` for `levyGoldPerUnit × units` Gold (60): its military units
become the Patron's for 20 turns (then return). Event `patronChanged`. First player to meet a
minor gets 1 free envoy there.

### 12.3 Quests
Each minor offers one **quest** at a time to every player who has met it (rules data; M2 list:
"clear the raider camp at X", "send a Trader to us", "build a Library", "meet N civs",
"have N envoys anywhere"). Completing gives +1 envoy there. A new quest every 20 turns or on
completion.

### 12.4 War with minors
Declaring war on a minor (`declareWar(on:)` works on minors) costs every envoy there, gives −10
relationship with every major who has envoys there, and the minor's Patron (if any) may choose to
join (AI: joins if its army ≥ the attacker's estimate). Conquering a minor's city makes it a normal
city of the conqueror (captured rules), and the minor is eliminated. Minors never win and are not
counted for domination.

### 12.5 Free Cities
A fixed non-player faction (`kind: .free`) that owns revolted cities (§5.5). It takes no phase;
its cities produce one defender of the world era every 10 turns (max 2) and bombard; never
moves units out. Always at war with everyone; no diplomacy.

## 13. Trade

- **Trader** (§8.1) built in a city; `startTradeRoute(unit, to: city)` with a city of its owner
  (domestic) or of a major at peace with open borders, or a minor (international), within
  `routeRange` (15 land tiles via a path of the Trader's knowledge; +5 with Cartography for sea
  routes between cities with Harbors). The Trader travels; the route starts when it arrives and
  lasts `routeTurns` (30); then it is idle in the destination and can start another.
- **Capacity:** routes running ≤ 1 + Markets + *Foreign Trade* + Guilds + Merchant Republic.
- **Yields** per turn for the origin city (the destination's owner also gets the listed share):

| Route | Origin gets | Destination gets |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic | +2 Food, +1 Prod; +1 Food per Granary at destination; +1 Prod per Workshop at destination | — |
| International (major) | +3 Gold, +1 per Market/Bank at destination; +1 Science if the destination knows 2+ techs the origin's owner does not | +1 Gold |
| Minor | +4 Gold; +1 envoy every 15 turns with the minor | — |

- **Roads:** each route lays a road on every land tile of its path at once (§3.3).
- **Plunder:** an enemy (or raider) military unit on a route tile at war with the owner may
  `plunder` it: route ends, +`plunderGold` (40) Gold, Trader removed.
- **Knowledge:** routes are visible to whoever sees their tiles (a faint line, §18.3).

## 14. Diplomacy basics (M2-D9)

### 14.1 Relationship score
Per ordered pair of majors (A's opinion of B), an integer from itemized **modifiers** (rules
data), each with an amount and a decay (per turn toward 0), shown to the player exactly as the AI
uses them (SPEC pillar: transparency):

| Modifier | Amount | Decay |
|---|---|---|
| First met | +5 | 1 / 5 turns |
| Shared border (any tile within 2 of B's territory) | −2 per city pair, max −10 | while true |
| Settled near us (B founded a city within 8 of A's city) | −10 | 1 / 3 turns |
| Surprise war / formal war on A | −30 / −10 | 1 / 4 turns |
| War on A's friend | −10 | 1 / 4 turns |
| Declared friends | +15 | while friends |
| Denounced | −15 | while denounced |
| Trade route between us | +2 per route, max +6 | while running |
| Deal kept to the end | +3 | 1 / 10 turns |
| Same Patron competition (both with envoys at a minor, B is Patron) | −3 per minor | while true |
| Civ agenda (§15) | ±10 | while true |

Levels: ≤ −30 **Unfriendly**, −29…+29 **Neutral**, ≥ +30 **Friendly** (shown as a face icon).

### 14.2 Friendship and denouncement
- `proposeFriendship(to:)`: needs ≥ +30 opinion on both sides for the AI to accept, costs 10
  Influence (−50% with *Charismatic Leader*), lasts 30 turns, renewable; while friends: no war
  between them, +1 trade route capacity for routes between them. Unlocked by *Diplomatic Service*.
- `denounce(player)`: free; enables a formal war 5 turns later; lasts 20 turns; ends Friendship.

### 14.3 Deals
`proposeDeal(to:, Deal)`, `respondToDeal(from:, accept:)` (same phase rules as M1 peace proposals:
answered in the target's phase; declined → 5-turn cool-down; the AI answers at once in its next
phase). A `Deal` has items on each side:
- Gold (lump), Gold per turn (for 30 turns), a luxury or strategic resource (one source, 30 turns),
  open borders (30 turns), a city (peace deals only, never a capital), peace (with a war).
- **AI evaluation:** each item has a value for each side (rules data: gold 1:1, gold/turn × 30 ×
  80%, luxury the AI lacks 120 Gold else 40, open borders 30 if not planning war on them else ∞,
  city 150 + 30 × pop + 20 × buildings); opinion adjusts the price (Friendly −15%, Unfriendly
  +25%); accept when its side's value ≥ 0. **Rejections explain** the gap: "They want about 40
  more Gold" (rounded to 10), using the same function. `dealPreview(Deal)` is a PlayerView query
  so the UI shows "would accept" before sending.
- A deal broken by war (open borders, gold per turn) ends at once; the breaker gets −15 with
  every civ that met both.

| Mongols (Genghis Khan) | **Örtöö:** trade routes to a city reveal it and its tiles within 2 for 20 turns; cavalry +3 Str vs any civ you have a trade route with; cavalry +1 move | **Keshig** (extra): ranged cavalry, may move after attacking | **Ordu** (replaces Stable): cavalry +1 move more, +15 XP | Likes civs with big armies; dislikes civs with small armies near it |
| Mali (Mansa Musa) | **Songs of the Jeli:** city centres +1 Food, +1 Gold per adjacent desert or desert floodplain; Gold buys cost −20%; −30% production toward buildings (not units, not quarters) | **Mandekalu Cavalry** (replaces Knight): own civilians within 1 cannot be captured; +25 Gold on a kill | **Suguba** (replaces Market): also −10% on Gold buys in that city; +2 Gold | Likes civs with more Gold income than it; dislikes civs that trade with its rivals |
| China (Wu Zetian) | **Dynastic Cycle:** Insights give 50% (not 40%); Builders +1 charge; Culture +1 in cities with an Amphitheater | **Crouching Tiger** (replaces Crossbowman): 50 ranged, range 1 | **Great Wall segment** (improvement, Masonry): on a border tile, +4 defence, +1 Gold, +1 Culture with Castles; adjacent segments +1 Gold | Likes civs that research the same era; dislikes civs ahead in techs |

(they use the generic list and an empty agenda).

## 16. Victory and defeat

M1 §10 stands with:
- **Turn limit 150** (M2-D1; `constants.turnLimit`).
- **Score** = 5·cities + population + 3·techs + 2·civics + 2·buildings + 3·quarters + 2·envoys +
  `wonders` (none) — the Victory screen shows each civ's breakdown.
- **Domination** counts only majors; minors and Free Cities are ignored.
- **Elimination:** a major with no cities and no Settlers. A minor with no city.

## 17. AI (GameAI)

M1 §13's principles stand: decisions only from `state.view(for:)`, changes only through
`GameEngine.apply`, randomness only from the AI stream, integer scoring, **stateless between
phases**, the fairness test. M2 restructures the AI into the four layers of SPEC §12.1, each a
pure function of the view (plus the layer above's output for this phase).

### 17.1 Grand strategy (every phase, cheap)
Picks `focus ∈ {expansion, science, culture, military}` and a weight vector for the lower layers.
Inputs: civ agenda and ability (rules data: per-civ prior weights, e.g. Mongols military 140),
land left (good sites), relative power (`army / estimate` per met rival), relative science and
culture (from visible scores), threats. Hysteresis without memory: the **current** focus is
read back from the state (the government and policies the AI slotted for it), and a focus
changes only when another scores ≥ 120% of it.

### 17.2 Economy
- **Research and civics:** utility = value of what the node unlocks under the focus weights
  (units by power per cost vs the best known, buildings and quarters by expected yield across own
  cities, governments by slot value) + 50% bonus for a node whose Insight already fired. The M1
  fixed orders become the tie-break only.
- **Government and policies:** greedy fill of slots by policy utility (rules data per policy and
  focus), re-evaluated on each civic completion (free swap) only.
- **City builds:** M1's priorities (defence quota, escorts, Settlers, Builders) stay first; then
  utility over buildings, quarters (placed with the same adjacency scorer as the UI suggestions,
  §18.2), Traders (route value from §13 over best destination), army units. Never queue an item
  whose upkeep would push net gold below 0 (M1 rule, with building upkeep).
- **Stability and Loyalty:** a city projected below Content queues an Arena/Monument first or
  slots *Retainers*; a city projected to flip within 5 turns gets a garrison and (if it pays)
  *Praetorium*/*Magistrates*. Settling scores subtract predicted foreign pressure (§5.5) at the
  site: −1 per 20 foreign P.
- **Builders:** resources first (luxuries the player lacks > strategic it needs > bonus), then
  M1 order; build roads between own cities 2–6 apart when idle.
- **Envoys:** each token goes to the minor where it completes a tier or takes/keeps Patron, else
  the one whose type matches the focus.
- **Diplomacy layer:** accepts/proposes deals by §14.3; proposes Friendship to Friendly civs it
  does not plan war on; denounces the war target 5 turns before declaring when posture is
  `prepare` (formal war instead of surprise).

### 17.3 Military operations and influence maps
`InfluenceMaps` (PlayerView query, arrays per tile, computed once per AI phase):
`danger` (M1's `dangerMap`), `ownPower` (power of own units projected 1 phase), `frontline`
(tiles where both are > 0), `territory` (own/foreign/unowned distance field). Operations:
`defendCity(c)`, `attackCity(c)`, `escort(civilian)`, `clearCamp(camp)`, `navalPatrol(area)`.
Each phase: list candidate operations with a utility (threat × city value for defence;
target value × win chance for attack, where win chance comes from summed combat previews of
the planned army vs the target's estimate; camp gold and Insight for camps), assign free units
greedily by distance and class fit, keep the M1 rally/stage/advance logic inside `attackCity`.
M1's war test becomes the `attackCity` utility threshold.

### 17.4 Tactics
M1 §13 tactics stand, plus: siege units move adjacent to an escorted tile and fire next phase;
cavalry pick off wounded or ranged units and retreat; naval ranged units bombard coastal cities
in range; pillage enemy improvements when nothing better (preview value ≤ 0 everywhere);
promotions chosen by class priority (rules data); upgrades when gold ≥ 2 × cost and the unit is
in a city.

### 17.5 Budgets
AI phase ≤ 80 ms (max) on Duel and ≤ 400 ms on Standard 1+5 at turn 150 (Mac, release); minors
and raiders ≤ 5 ms per phase each. SPEC §12.3's device budget stands.

## 19. HeadlessSim and balance

- `--ai` games gain per player: era reached by turn, techs and civics known, government, policies
  slotted, quarters (by kind, mean adjacency), Housing-capped cities, Stability state counts,
  loyalty flips (lost/gained), trade routes, envoys and Patronages, raider camps cleared, units
  lost to raiders, upgrades, promotions, deals made and rejected; world-era turns; Free Cities.
- `--p0 newbie` plays M2 systems with simple rules (Campus first quarter, default policy fill,
  envoys to the nearest minor).
- New `make` targets: `balance-m2` (Standard, 20 seeds, 150 turns) and `loyalty` (prints flips and
  their causes).

**M2 balance targets** (Standard 1+5, 20 seeds, 150 turns, unless noted):
- pacing table §2 met;
- every AI ≥ 4 cities by turn 80 in ≥ 80% of games; a war by turn 80 in ≥ 70%; a city captured by
  turn 150 in ≥ 50%;
- **Loyalty:** 0–3 flips per game on average (SPEC §5.5); no flip of a city within 6 of its
  owner's capital; a city founded ≤ 5 from a larger rival's core flips within 40 turns in ≥ 50% of
  cases when unsupported;
- **Stability:** at turn 100, ≤ 15% of AI cities below Content; ≤ 2% in Revolt;
- **Housing:** at turn 100, median AI city within 1 of its Housing (growth is capped, not stalled);
- **Raiders:** newbie seat loses no city to raiders by turn 100 (40 Duel seeds); camps cleared
  per game ≥ 3;
- **Minors:** every minor has a Patron by turn 100 in ≥ 60% of games; ≤ 1 minor conquered per game
  on average;
- **Civs:** across 40 Duel games per pair of the M2 four, no civ wins more than 60% against any
  other (ability balance);
- **Trade:** AIs run ≥ 2 routes by turn 80;
- no crashes; deterministic; invariants hold every phase with `--check-every-phase` on 5 seeds.

## 20. Architecture

### 20.1 Modules
Unchanged layout (M1 §12.1). GameCore grows new files per system (`Resources.swift`,
`Quarters.swift`, `Stability.swift`, `Loyalty.swift`, `Civics.swift`, `Policies.swift`,
`Trade.swift`, `Minors.swift`, `Raiders.swift`, `Diplomacy.swift`, `Promotions.swift`); GameAI adds
`AIGrandStrategy`, `AIOperations`, `InfluenceMaps` (a GameCore query), `MinorAI`, `RaiderAI`.

### 20.2 Rules data
- One `ruleset.json` (id `m2.1`) with new sections: `eras`, `resources`, `quarters`, `civics`,
  `governments`, `policies`, `promotions`, `insights` (inside techs/civics), `raiders`, `minors`,
  `trade`, `diplomacy`, `civs[].ability`, `stability`, `housing`, `loyalty`. Integers and arrays
  only, percentages as `…Pct` (M1 §12.2).
- New string-backed IDs: `CivicID`, `GovernmentID`, `PolicyID`, `PromotionID`, `QuarterKind`
  (enum, procedural art), `Resource` (enum, raw values append-only), `MinorType` (enum).
- Effects are a **closed list of effect kinds** (`yield(kind, amount, scope)`, `strength(class,
  amount, condition)`, `costPct(itemFilter, pct)`, `housing`, `stability`, `loyalty`,
  `adjacencyMultiplier`, `routeCapacity`, `moves`, …) with typed scopes and conditions, shared by
  policies, governments, buildings, civ abilities and minor bonuses. Validation rejects unknown
  kinds, dangling references, cycles in tech and civic graphs, unreachable unlocks.

### 20.3 State
New `GameState` fields (no `Set`/`Dictionary`, sorted arrays, as M1 §12.3): `worldEra`,
`raiderCamps`, `tradeRoutes`, `minorQuests`, `nextRouteID`; `Player`: `kind (.major/.minor/.free/
.raiders)`, civics (known, current, target, per-item progress), research per-item progress,
`government`, `policies`, `stockpiles`, `influence`, `envoyTokens`, `envoysSent`, `warWeariness`,
`opinions` (per other major: modifier list), friendships, denouncements, deals in force;
`City`: `quarters [{kind, tile, buildings, pillaged}]`, `loyalty`, `freeCityPressure`,
`tilesBought`, specialists; `Tile`: `resource`, `road`, `pillaged`; `Unit`: `promotions`,
`embarked` (derived, not stored), `levyUntil`, `tradeRoute`.

Player indices: majors first (human 0), then minors, then the Free Cities, then Raiders. Phase
order follows the index; Free Cities take no phase. `EventRecord.audience` widens to `UInt32`
(Large: 8 majors + 8 minors + 2).

### 20.4 Commands and events
New commands: `setCivic`, `setGovernment`, `setPolicies`, `purchaseTile`, `promote`, `upgrade`,
`pillage`, `plunder`, `buildRoad`, `startTradeRoute`, `sendEnvoy`, `levy`, `proposeFriendship`,
`respondToFriendship`, `denounce`, `proposeDeal`, `respondToDeal`; `setQueue` accepts
`quarter(kind, at:)`; `proposePeace` takes optional terms.
New events: `worldEraAdvanced`, `insightGained`, `civicCompleted`, `governmentChanged`,
`quarterCompleted`, `quarterPillaged`, `stabilityChanged(city, state)`, `loyaltyWarning`,
`cityRevolted`, `cityJoined`, `resourceRevealed`, `promoted`, `upgraded`, `raiderCampCreated`
(audience: players who see it), `raiderCampCleared`, `tradeRouteStarted/Ended`, `envoySent`,
`patronChanged`, `questCompleted`, `friendshipDeclared`, `denounced`, `dealProposed/Accepted/
Declined`.

### 20.5 Determinism and performance
New salts: resources `0x2E50`, raiders `0x2A1D`, minors placement `0x3171`, quests `0x3172`.
Every new system is integer-only and ordered by id or tile index. Budgets: Stability + Loyalty for
all cities ≤ 1 ms per phase on Large; influence maps ≤ 5 ms per AI phase on Standard; a full
Standard 150-turn AI game ≤ 60 s in HeadlessSim (release, Mac).

### 20.6 Persistence (M2-D4)
Format version bump; schema fingerprint updated; M1 saves listed greyed ("made with an older
version"). Golden saves for M2 taken at turns 1, 60, 140.

## 22. Work plan

M2 is too big for one integration. It lands in **five increments**, each merged, balanced with
HeadlessSim and playable before the next starts. Within an increment, packages run in parallel
copies and merge by three-way merge as in M1 §19. Every increment starts with a **contracts**
package in the live tree (types, rules sections with empty or minimal data, stubs, events,
renderer stubs, save fields, re-pinned hashes).

|---|---|---|---|
| **2.1 Map and units** | resources, stockpiles, new improvements, roads, embarking, naval units, all 24 unit types (art), upgrades, promotions, siege/walls, Continents/Archipelago, 30-tech tree with Insights, world era | Core · Art · AI (resources, naval, siege, promotions) · App (tech tree, unit card) · Integration | The M1 game with a full tech tree to Medieval plays through turn 150 without empty research |
